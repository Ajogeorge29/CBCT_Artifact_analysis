Executive finding

The workbook holds 72 values per site in three 24-measurement trial blocks. The manuscripts describe 24 values per site. Nine of ten scan means reproduce at two decimals from trial 1; Scan F is 632.2047 in the workbook (632.20 at two decimals) but 632.21 in both manuscripts. All nine Scan A p-values reproduce as a ten-group Tukey HSD on trial 1. That identifies the prior calculation but leaves two-thirds of the stored measurements unexplained.

All four like-for-like alginate comparisons move toward higher mean PIV in all three trials. This is descriptively consistent, but the PIV locations are nested technical readings within scans; they are not automatically independent experimental replicates.

Corrected study framing

Revised objective. To characterize and compare peri-implant CBCT gray-value distributions at standardized sampling locations around titanium implants in matched scan configurations acquired with and without alginate, and to assess consistency across three trials and tray status.

Revised hypothesis. The null hypothesis is that matched alginate and no-alginate scans have the same peri-implant gray-value distribution. The research hypothesis is that the distributions differ. Direction and magnitude are estimated rather than presupposed. Gray-value differences are a surrogate imaging outcome and do not by themselves demonstrate artifact reduction or worsening.

The study remains a CBCT metal-artifact study. Optical scanning, dimensional accuracy, and material shrinkage are outside the dataset and are not interpreted.

Risks requiring resolution

  1. Incomplete schema: define trial, radius, direction, repeat/slice, observer, unit, and zero semantics.
  2. Endpoint selection: explain why trial 1 was primary or reanalyze all trials using a stated repeated-measures model.
  3. Experimental unit: PIV points within a scan are correlated; identify independent skull/scan/implant replication.
  4. Confounding: scan IDs change implant number/location and alginate; Scan A also removes the implant.
  5. Audit trail: link CBCT IDs, ROI/slice coordinates, extraction code/version, and qualitative scores.
  6. Reporting: use p < 0.001, not p = 0.000, and report effects with intervals.

Recommended remediation

  1. Freeze a signed-off data dictionary and preserve the workbook as an immutable private source.
  2. Reconcile all three trials with the manuscript endpoint before further inference.
  3. Use planned matched contrasts and a justified hierarchical/repeated model only when independent replication supports it.
  4. Document and reproduce the E–F, G–H, and I–J p-value method.
  5. Add blinded qualitative ratings and a justified inter-rater agreement analysis.
  6. Archive the final environment, source checksum, software versions, and analysis log.
