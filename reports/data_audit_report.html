Executive finding

The workbook holds 72 values per site in three unlabelled 24-value blocks. The manuscripts describe 24 values per site. Nine of ten scan means reproduce at two decimals from block 1; Scan F is 632.2047 in the workbook (632.20 at two decimals) but 632.21 in both manuscripts. All nine Scan A p-values reproduce as a ten-group Tukey HSD on block 1. That identifies the prior calculation but leaves two-thirds of the stored measurements unexplained.

All four like-for-like alginate comparisons move toward higher mean PIV in all three blocks. This is descriptively consistent, but the PIV locations are nested technical readings within scans; they are not automatically independent experimental replicates.

Risks requiring resolution

  1. Unknown schema: define block, radius, direction, repeat/slice, observer, unit, and zero semantics.
  2. Endpoint selection: explain why block 1 was primary or reanalyze all blocks using a stated repeated-measures model.
  3. Experimental unit: PIV points within a scan are correlated; identify independent skull/scan/implant replication.
  4. Confounding: scan IDs change implant number/location and alginate; Scan A also removes the implant.
  5. Audit trail: link CBCT IDs, ROI/slice coordinates, extraction code/version, and qualitative scores.
  6. Reporting: use p < 0.001, not p = 0.000, and report effects with intervals.

Recommended remediation

  1. Freeze a signed-off data dictionary and preserve the workbook as an immutable private source.
  2. Reconcile all three blocks with the manuscript endpoint before further inference.
  3. Use planned matched contrasts and a justified hierarchical/repeated model only when independent replication supports it.
  4. Document and reproduce the E–F, G–H, and I–J p-value method.
  5. Add blinded qualitative ratings and a justified inter-rater agreement analysis.
  6. Archive the final environment, source checksum, software versions, and analysis log.
